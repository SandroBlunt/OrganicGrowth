# Script v2 — PLAIN "EXPLAIN-IT-LIKE-I'M-NEW" STYLE
**Straw Motion · "AI's Week, Unhypped"** — read aloud, ~6–7 min.
Voice: warm, patient, zero jargon, plain analogies, no math. Each story ends on **how it helps you** (business / marketing / getting into AI). Shots → `shot-list.md`.

---

**[SHOT 1]** This week, computers got much better at making videos, pictures, and voices. I'll walk you through it slowly, in plain words — and after each one I'll tell you how it's actually useful, whether you run a business, do marketing, or you're just getting into AI. No tech background needed.

This is Straw Motion. Let's start.

**[SHOT 2]** First, Alibaba in China released a video maker called **Wan 3.0**. The clever part: **[SHOT 3]** you give it a normal document — a report, a slideshow, even a website link — and it turns that into a short video with sound, about thirty seconds long. You don't write a script.
**How that helps you:** if you have a business, you can turn something you already wrote — a case study, a one-pager — into a little promo video, without hiring anyone. *(One honest note: we could only confirm the details from one website, so double-check before you rely on the exact numbers.)*

**[SHOT 4]** Next, ByteDance released **Seedance 2.5**. It makes thirty-second videos with sound, and **[SHOT 5]** the characters now keep the same face from shot to shot — which used to be a real problem.
**How that helps you:** **[SHOT 6]** you can make short ads where your product or your character looks the same in every clip. For a marketer, that consistency is the whole point.

**[SHOT 7]** Third, **MiniMax H3**. Its trick is making the video and the sound at the same time, together. It's more of a tinkerer's kit than a simple app.
**How that helps you:** if you want to offer AI video as a paid skill, this is the toolkit to start learning now — the people who learn it early get the early clients.

**[SHOT 8]** Fourth, **FLUX 3** from Black Forest Labs. **[SHOT 9]** It's one single tool that makes pictures, videos, and voices — all three. **[SHOT 10]** There's even a version that helps robots react quickly.
**How that helps you:** one tool for your whole content — the picture, the clip, and the voiceover. You learn one thing instead of three.

So that's four video tools in one week, all now making sound and picture together.

**[SHOT 11]** Let's switch to still pictures. Microsoft made its own picture-drawing AI, **MAI-Image-2.6**, and it came second in a public contest where people vote on the best images.
**How that helps you:** more good options means the pictures you need for your business get cheaper — and this one sits inside Microsoft's tools many companies already use.

**[SHOT 12]** Now, headphones on. Three labs made talking assistants that reply in under half a second — about as fast as a person, with no awkward pause.
**How that helps you:** if you take calls or bookings, you can have a voice assistant that actually feels like talking to someone. And building these is a real, in-demand job right now.

**[SHOT 13]** Two bigger ones to finish. **Meta** gave away a powerful AI for free, small enough to run on a single gaming graphics card.
**How that helps you:** you can run capable AI on your own computer — private, and with no bill per use. For a small business that saves real money; for your career, it's a skill people want.

**[SHOT 14]** Last, Google released **Gemini 3.7 Flash** and cut its price in half.
**How that helps you:** if an AI idea felt too expensive before, it might be affordable now. The theme of the week: these tools keep getting cheaper.

**[SHOT 15]** That's the week — what happened, and how each piece can actually help you. Thanks for watching; we explain the AI news in plain words every week. See you next time.

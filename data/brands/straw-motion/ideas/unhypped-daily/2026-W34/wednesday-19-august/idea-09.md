## Angle
The tension is between the standard criticism of free AI giveaways and the first evidence against it. The usual line is that a giveaway buys download counts and nothing else: users who never pay, a number for a slide. TechCrunch's headline says the free offer "left it with millions more users in India". The AI Insider's headline adds the part that actually matters, that the Airtel partnership "shows early signs of converting Indian users into paying customers". The contrast to ride: the metric everyone wrote off as vanity is starting to produce a revenue line, and the interesting number is the small one, not the big one.

## Hook Concept
The surprise is which number turned out to matter. Open on the giveaway everyone dismissed, the enormous free download figure, then the turn: some of those people are now paying. The reframe: the headline number was never the story, the share of it that opened a wallet is. Concept only, the writer lands the final line.

## Talking Points
- TechCrunch's headline is the plain result: Perplexity's "free AI offer left it with millions more users in India" (Aug 18).
- The AI Insider's headline supplies the commercial half: the free Airtel partnership "shows early signs of converting Indian users into paying customers" (Aug 18).
- The trend's own framing puts the giveaway at roughly 56 million free downloads, which is the scale that made this a talking point in the first place and the number every critic pointed at.
- Airtel is the mechanism, not a detail. Bundling a paid AI subscription into a mobile carrier's plan puts it in front of people who would never visit a pricing page, in a market where the carrier is the distribution channel.
- The founder read, and this is why it belongs on this Channel: the experiment here is about distribution, not about the product. The transferable lesson for a small business is where you put the offer, not which model you use.
- Say the sourcing out loud: no Perplexity statement and no Airtel statement appear in this evidence. Two outlets reported it on the same day, and that is the whole basis.

## The Catch (mandatory skeptic beat)
"Early signs of converting" is not a conversion rate, and no percentage appears anywhere in this evidence. A free-to-paid story with no number attached is a direction, not a result. There is also no first-party confirmation from either company, and a giveaway that ends will always produce some conversions purely from people who forgot to cancel. Until somebody publishes the rate and the churn, this stays an encouraging anecdote about distribution.

## Angle
The tension is between how loud the agent conversation is and how quiet the thing that would actually make agents work together was. Every week brings another argument about which agent is smartest. On Aug 18 Google put SAM, which MarkTechPost expands as Sovereign Agent Mesh, on GitHub, described as a zero-config, zero-trust peer-to-peer network for AI agents. No keynote, no blog post in the evidence, no launch video. The contrast to ride: the release that changes what agents can do together arrived as a repository nobody linked.

## Talking Points
- The primary source is the code itself: github.com/google/sam, published by Google. There is no accompanying Google blog post in this evidence, which is exactly why almost nobody saw it.
- MarkTechPost spells the name out and gives the one-line description: SAM is "Sovereign Agent Mesh", a "zero-config, zero-trust P2P network for AI agents" (Aug 18).
- Two of those terms carry the whole idea. "Peer to peer" means agents talk to each other directly, with no central server sitting in the middle taking a cut and keeping a log. "Zero trust" means each agent still has to prove who it is on every single request.
- "Zero config" is the claim worth testing, because it means an agent joins the mesh without a human hand-wiring the connection. That is the difference between a conference demo and something a two-person team can actually run.
- The builder read: if you have ever wired two agents together, the pain was never the model. It was the connection, the identity, and the permission. This targets exactly that layer, which is why it matters more than its coverage suggests.
- Sourcing honesty for the card: one repository and one write-up. Say so, and treat everything beyond the README as unverified.

## The Catch (mandatory skeptic beat)
This is a repository, not a product. Open sourcing something is not the same as shipping it, and Google has a long record of publishing agent tooling that never finds users. There is no adoption in this evidence, no named company running it, and no indication Google itself depends on it. One outlet covered it. Treat this as a well-aimed piece of plumbing that may or may not get connected to anything.

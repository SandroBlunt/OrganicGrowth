Everyone", is 6,000+ words that say "superintelligence" roughly 60 times. The shipped thing is Muse
Glimmer: 30 billion parameters, Apache 2.0, small enough to sit under 24GB of video memory, which means
one consumer graphics card in one desk. Every outlet is quoting the essay. The download is the story.
The tension to ride: the loudest part of Meta's day is a vision document, and the part that changes what
a viewer can do tonight is a file on Hugging Face nobody is reading the licence of.

## Talking points
- Muse Glimmer is a 30B-parameter open-weights agentic model released under an Apache 2.0 licence, and it
  fits in under 24GB of VRAM, so it runs on a single consumer GPU (MarkTechPost, Aug 10).
- Meta claims 3.1x faster decoding via "DFlash speculation". Decoding is just the model writing its
  answer out word chunk by word chunk, so this is a speed claim about how fast text appears.
- Zuckerberg's essay, "The Future is for Everyone", runs past 6,000 words (Fast Company counts ~6,500)
  and uses "superintelligent" about 60 times, per The Guardian's lead story.
- The essay also pledges $1 billion to communities that host Meta's data centers, and covers regulation,
  jobs, cybersecurity, bioweapon risk and surveillance (Fast Company's six-things summary).
- Meta promised to open-weight the bigger Muse Spark 1.2 "in the next few weeks". That one has not
  shipped. Today you can only download the 30B (Ars Technica).
- Hugging Face's own blog framed the release as "local, agentic, multimodal, and open source", which is
  the practical read: an agent that runs on your machine instead of someone's API.
- FT's headline is that Zuckerberg is attacking "closed" AI rivals, so this is positioning as much as
  engineering. Ars Technica calls it "another reboot of its struggling AI strategy".
- The builder read: if you shelved a local-agent project because the good models were API-only, the
  hardware bar for a capable 30B agent is now one 24GB card.

## The catch (mandatory skeptic beat)
Open weights is not open source. You get the finished brain as a file, not the data it was trained on and
not the recipe. 24GB is a high-end gaming card, not a laptop, so "runs locally" still means buying or
renting real hardware. And the model everyone actually wants, Muse Spark 1.2, is a promise with a
"few weeks" attached, from a company Ars Technica just described as rebooting its AI strategy again.

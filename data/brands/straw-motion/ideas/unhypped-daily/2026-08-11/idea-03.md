## Angle
The chip company that sells the shovels is now helping arrange the loan you use to buy them. Apollo,
Blackstone, Goldman Sachs and others are assembling roughly $500 billion of financing for AI
infrastructure alongside Nvidia, and FT called it plainly: "Nvidia becomes the bank of AI". The tension
for a normal viewer is that every AI announcement this year sounds like money arriving, and this one is
money being borrowed. Half a trillion dollars of debt is a bet that the demand shows up later, and the
company with the most to gain from that bet is helping put it together.

## Hook concept
Open on the role swap, not the number: the world's most valuable chipmaker is turning into a lender to
its own customers. The surprise is that a $500 billion "investment in AI" headline is a loan, and loans
have to be paid back by revenue that has not been earned yet. (Concept only, writer lands the final line.)

## Suggested Recipe
Both, per the Format. **News Carousel (7 slides)** because named lenders plus one giant number is a
card-per-fact swipe, and one slide can carry a single big "$500,000,000,000" as the hero. **News Short
Script (45 to 60s)** works with the stupidly-simple line up front: this is a mortgage, not a paycheck.
Data centers are the house, and the rent has to cover it.

## Talking points
- Apollo, Blackstone, Goldman Sachs and other lenders are working with Nvidia to assemble a financing
  package worth roughly $500 billion for AI infrastructure (FT, Aug 10).
- FT's lead framing is the role change: "Nvidia becomes the bank of AI". The chipmaker is moving from
  seller to financier.
- FT's second piece names the partners directly: "Wall Street giants partner with Nvidia on $500bn AI
  financing deal".
- NYT ran the same figure the same day as "Wall St. Wants Another Half-Trillion Dollars for the A.I.
  Boom". The word "another" is doing the work: this is on top of what is already committed.
- Plain-language version of what a financing package is: it is not Nvidia's cash and it is not customer
  revenue. It is borrowed money raised against the expectation that AI demand keeps growing.
- The you-first read: this is the money that pays for the data centers behind the tools you use, so the
  price of AI services over the next few years follows the interest on this, not the cost of the chips.
- The signal to actually watch is whether the package closes and on what terms, because reported
  assemblies and signed deals are different objects.

## The catch (mandatory skeptic beat)
This is reported as being assembled, not signed, and no terms have been disclosed in the coverage. More
importantly, when the supplier helps finance the buyer, demand looks stronger than it is: the same
dollars can appear as a loan on one side and a sale on the other. That does not make it wrong, and it is
not evidence of a bubble on its own. It just means $500 billion of appetite is not $500 billion of
proven demand, and neither FT nor NYT says it is.

  visibly the subject rather than an abstract chip icon.
- Real Apollo, Blackstone and Goldman Sachs logos, or their actual New York offices, on the lenders
  slide. These three names must be seen, not just mentioned.
- Real headline crops as proof: FT's "Nvidia becomes the bank of AI" and NYT's "Wall St. Wants Another
  Half-Trillion Dollars for the A.I. Boom", side by side.
- One slide where the number is the hero: "$500,000,000,000" rendered big, with "roughly, and not yet
  signed" as the small honest line under it.
- A real data-center construction site photo for the "what the money buys" slide, not a stock server room.
- Optional comparison chart only if the producer can verify the comparison figure from a source. Do not
  invent a scale comparison.
- CTA direction: ask whether viewers read a $500bn loan as confidence or as exposure. Fresh phrasing,
  never a recycled swipe prompt.

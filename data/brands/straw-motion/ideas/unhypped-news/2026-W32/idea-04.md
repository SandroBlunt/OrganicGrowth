## Angle
Skip the usual US-versus-China framing and look at what the benchmark measures. Alibaba's open-weights
Qwen3.8-Max scored 53.4 to GPT-5.6 Sol's 45.4 on JobBench, a test aimed at agentic tasks rather than
chat, and the same release ships a cheaper 27B variant. The tension: chat leaderboards tell you how a
model talks, JobBench is trying to tell you whether it can finish work, and on that one the downloadable
model is currently ahead of the flagship you rent. The honest counterweight is that 53.4 versus 45.4 is a
real gap, not a different league, and one benchmark is not your workload.

## Hook concept
Open on the switch in what is being measured. Everyone quotes chat benchmarks; this one scores whether a
model can complete a job's worth of tasks, and the model in front is the one you can download for free.
Name Qwen3.8-Max and both numbers up front. (Concept only, writer lands the final line.)

## Suggested Recipe
News Carousel (7 slides). Two scores and a "what the test actually measures" explanation are made for
card-sized statements, and the 27B variant gives the closing card a concrete next step.

## Talking points
- Qwen3.8-Max, from Alibaba, is open-weights and scored 53.4 against GPT-5.6 Sol's 45.4 on JobBench, a
  benchmark aimed at agentic tasks rather than conversation (Qwen's own blog).
- Open weights means downloadable: no per-token bill, no vendor lock-in, and it runs where you put it.
- The same release ships a cheaper 27B variant, so the family scales down to hardware a small team can
  actually afford to run.
- Un-hyped caveat: this is one test with one scoring setup. An 8-point gap is a real gap, not a knockout,
  and neither score is your codebase, your data, or your customers.
- Context for the same week: OpenAI cut Luna API pricing 80%, to $0.20 per million input tokens, so cheap
  is being attacked from the closed side and the open side at once (OpenAI's price-performance post).
- What to do with it: if you parked an agent idea on cost or on capability, this is a good week to retest
  it against one open model, one small 27B run, and one cheap API tier, then compare on your own tasks
  rather than on anyone's leaderboard.

# idea-10 — "A chipmaker posted record profits and $590 billion vanished in 48 hours"

- **Brand:** straw-motion

- **Rides trend:** trend-07 — AI infrastructure jitters, the chip market wobble
- **Fit Score:** 0.59

## Angle
SK Hynix reported record profits. South Korea's stock market then halted trading twice inside 48 hours
and roughly $590 billion in market value was erased. The tension: nothing failed. Record profits simply
came in under sky-high analyst expectations, and the market was priced for more. That is a story about
what people expected from the AI buildout, not about whether AI works, and telling those two apart is the
whole job of an unhypped-news post.

## Hook concept
Open on the contradiction with the two numbers side by side: record profits, and about $590 billion gone
in two days, with trading halted twice. The surprise is that the trigger was good results that were not
good enough. (Concept only, writer lands the final line.)

## Suggested Recipe
News Carousel (7 slides). One number per card carries this well, and the "expectations versus results"
flip is a strong middle-of-carousel turn without needing a chart.

## Talking points
- SK Hynix reported record profits that still fell short of elevated analyst expectations (CNBC).
- South Korea's stock market halted trading twice within 48 hours, and roughly $590 billion in market
  value was erased (CNBC).
- The plain read: this was an expectations reset, not a results failure. Record was not record enough
  against what the AI trade had already priced in.
- What is genuinely being questioned is whether AI infrastructure spending keeps paying back this fast,
  which is a different question from whether the technology is useful.
- Worth holding next to the rest of the week: OpenAI cut its API pricing 80% in the same window, so the
  cost of using AI and the cost of building the machines that run it are moving in opposite directions.
- Un-hyped bottom line: one bad expectations week is not a verdict. The thing to watch is whether the
  spending plans themselves change, not the share price.

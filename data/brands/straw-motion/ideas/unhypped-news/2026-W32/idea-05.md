## Angle
Black Forest Labs put FLUX 3 into early access as one foundation model trained across images, video and
audio, generating 20-second multilingual video with native audio instead of three separate tools stitched
together. Then the same lab shipped FLUX-mimic with mimic robotics, a video-action model that controls
real robots with 101ms reaction times. The tension: we file "AI video tools" and "robotics" as different
industries, and this says they are the same recipe. A model that predicts what happens next in a video
can also decide what a hand does next.

## Hook concept
Open on the collision: the family of models people use to make an AI clip is also driving physical robot
manipulation at 101 milliseconds. The surprise is that generating video and controlling a robot turn out
to be the same problem wearing two outfits. (Concept only, writer lands the final line.)

## Talking points
- FLUX 3 is in early access as a single foundation model trained across images, video and audio, rather
  than three separate models bolted together (Black Forest Labs' own post).
- It generates 20-second multilingual video with native audio, meaning sound produced with the video
  instead of dubbed on afterwards (BFL).
- FLUX-mimic, built with mimic robotics, is a video-action model for general-purpose robot manipulation
  with 101ms reaction times (BFL).
- Creator read: one model covering image, video and sound removes handoffs. No separate voice pass, no
  separate audio tool, for a 20-second cut.
- The bigger idea in plain terms: guessing the next frame of a video and choosing a robot's next move are
  the same kind of prediction, which is why one model family does both.
- Un-hyped caveat: FLUX 3 is early access, and 20 seconds with native audio is a clip, not a film. Plan
  around the length limit before you promise a client a finished piece.

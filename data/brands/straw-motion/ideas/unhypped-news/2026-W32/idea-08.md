## Angle
Five chapters landed inside one week. 77 companies including Google, Meta, OpenAI and Nvidia signed a
letter opposing US restrictions on downloadable models. Moonshot AI then released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion
parameter open-weight model with a 1M-token context, free on Hugging Face. US officials accused Moonshot
of using Anthropic's Fable without authorization to build it, with sanctions floated. AI experts publicly
disputed that, pointing to reinforcement-learning advances instead. Officials then backed off restricting
Chinese open models in favour of audits and benchmarks. The tension: the panic headline and the correction
arrived in the same news cycle, and only one of them travelled.

## Hook concept
Open on the whiplash, not the politics: the same week the US floated sanctioning a Chinese AI model, its
own experts said the accusation did not hold up and officials quietly switched to audits. The surprise is
how fast the scary version was walked back. Name Kimi K3 up front. (Concept only, writer lands the final
line.)

## Suggested Recipe
News Carousel (7 slides). A five-beat sequence with a reversal is the clearest possible case for a
swipe-through timeline, one chapter per card.

## Talking points
- 77 companies including Google, Meta, OpenAI and Nvidia signed "Open Weights and American AI Leadership,"
  opposing US restrictions on open and downloadable models. Nvidia's Jensen Huang backed it publicly.
- Days later Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 open-weight on Hugging Face: 2.8 trillion parameters and a
  1M-token context window, free to download.
- US officials alleged Moonshot used Anthropic's Fable model without authorization to build K3, with
  sanctions floated (Yahoo News).
- AI experts publicly disputed the accusation, pointing to genuine reinforcement-learning advances as the
  reason K3 is good (TechCrunch).
- Officials then backed off restricting Chinese open models, favouring audits and benchmarks instead
  (The Neuron).
- The builder read: the rules about what you are allowed to download are being written in real time, and
  this week they moved toward testing models rather than banning them. Worth tracking if any part of your
  stack runs on open weights.

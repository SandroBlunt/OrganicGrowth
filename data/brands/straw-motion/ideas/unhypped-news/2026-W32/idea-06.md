# idea-06 — "Hollywood sent legal letters over 15-second AI clips. The new version makes 3 minutes."

## Angle
ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 generates video up to 3 minutes with synced audio and accepts 50+ references,
including 3D models. Its predecessor topped out at 15 seconds, and that 15-second version was already
enough to draw cease-and-desist letters from Hollywood studios. The tension: the capability and the legal
heat grew in the same step. At 15 seconds it was a novelty clip; at 3 minutes with sound it is a finished
explainer or a product spot, which is the length where somebody's rights and somebody's business model
actually collide.

## Hook concept
Open on the jump in scale against the legal backdrop: studios objected to a tool that made 15-second
clips, and the next release makes 3 minutes with synced audio, roughly twelve times longer. The surprise
is the direction of travel while the letters are still in flight. (Concept only, writer lands the final
line.)

## Suggested Recipe
News Carousel (7 slides). "Here is what it does / here is what it means for your work / here is the open
legal question" is a natural swipe, and the reference-image mechanic is easier to show as cards than to
narrate.

## Talking points
- Seedance 2.5 generates video up to 3 minutes with synced audio, up from a 15-second predecessor
  (ByteDance's own announcement).
- It accepts 50+ references, including 3D models, which is how you hold the same character, product or
  set steady across shots (ByteDance).
- Hollywood studios sent cease-and-desist letters over the 15-second version, so the pushback started
  before the 3-minute one existed (noted in ByteDance's own announcement).
- Creator read: 3 minutes with synced audio is a full explainer or a product spot, not filler B-roll. The
  use case changes with the length, and so does the client conversation.
- The un-hyped part: what you feed the 50+ reference slots decides whether the output is your work or
  someone else's likeness, and that is precisely what the studios' letters are about.
- What to watch next: whether those letters turn into a court case or a licensing deal. That outcome, not
  the model's specs, sets what tools like this are allowed to do next.
